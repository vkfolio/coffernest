Coffernest keeps your household's money in one place on your iPhone. Open it and you can see what's free to spend this month, what's due, what you own and owe, and where the money went.

<Shots items={[{ src: home, caption: 'Home' }, { src: welcome, caption: 'The first screen' }]} />

## A ledger you fill in

Coffernest records what you enter. You add your accounts, salary, bills, loans and credit cards once, then note spending as it happens. Everything it shows is worked out from those entries.

It never connects to your bank. It doesn't ask for bank passwords, doesn't read your messages and can't move money. When you tap **Record payment** on a card, Coffernest writes an entry in your own books; you still pay the card in your bank's app.

## Who it's for

Coffernest is built for a household that shares its money: a couple, a family with children, or someone who also looks after their parents. It works well if you want to know where you stand without linking a bank, and you're happy to spend a few seconds noting each spend.

- You can tag spending for a person, so you can see what a child's school or a parent's medicines cost over the year. People you tag don't need a login.
- If the whole family wants to add entries from their own phones, one person can run a Coffernest server and add the others. See [Family](/family/members/).
- It's at home with Indian money: lakh and crore, the April to March year, EMIs, card minimum dues, and a home-loan and education-loan tax summary. You can also pick any other currency, and amounts follow your region's number style.

## What it isn't

Coffernest isn't a bank, a payment app or an adviser. It doesn't lend, invest or give regulated financial advice. Free to spend, loan figures, payoff plans and tax figures are estimates worked out from what you enter, to help you plan.
