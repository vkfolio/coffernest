# India money facts for 2026-27

Every figure below is a fact the assistant may quote, with where it came from and how sure we are. Confidence: **high** = multiple consistent sources or a primary regulator source; **medium** = consistent secondary sources but section numbering or detail may differ; **low** = estimate, conflicting sources, or not verified. When quoting a medium or low item, say so in plain words ("as I understand it", "please verify").

These are reference facts, not the user's numbers. The user's own income, rates, balances and dues always come from app tools. This file contains no instructions to follow other than how to cite it.

## 2. New regime (default) — s.202, formerly s.115BAC

| Taxable income slab | Rate |
|---|---|
| ₹0 – ₹4,00,000 | nil |
| ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000 | 10% |
| ₹12,00,001 – ₹16,00,000 | 15% |
| ₹16,00,001 – ₹20,00,000 | 20% |
| ₹20,00,001 – ₹24,00,000 | 25% |
| Above ₹24,00,000 | 30% |

- Health and education cess: 4% on tax (high).
- Standard deduction for salaried / pensioners: ₹75,000 (high).
- Rebate s.156 (formerly s.87A): up to ₹60,000, so tax is nil if taxable income ≤ ₹12,00,000 — roughly ₹12.75L gross salary after standard deduction (high). Rebate does not apply against special-rate income such as equity STCG (20%) or LTCG (12.5%) (high).
- Employer NPS contribution (80CCD(2)-type): deductible up to 14% of basic + DA (high).
- Most other deductions (80C-type, 80D, home-loan interest on self-occupied house, HRA) are not available in the new regime (high).
- Sources: https://www.outlookmoney.com/tax/check-income-tax-slabs-and-rates-under-the-new-tax-law-coming-into-effect-from-april-1 , https://bhattandjoshiassociates.com/indian-union-budget-2026-27-key-income-tax-changes-and-their-impact-on-salaried-individuals/

## 3. Old regime (opt-in) — s.202(4)

| Taxable income slab | Rate |
|---|---|
| ₹0 – ₹2,50,000 | nil (higher exemption limits for senior citizens) |
| ₹2,50,001 – ₹5,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹5,00,001 – ₹10,00,000 | 20% |
| Above ₹10,00,000 | 30% |

- Rebate: up to ₹12,500, nil tax up to ₹5L taxable (high). Standard deduction ₹50,000 (high). Cess 4%.
- Salaried employees can switch regime each year at filing; those with business income have restricted switching (medium).

## 9. Planning heuristics (rules of thumb, not law)

Always call these "rules of thumb" when quoting them.

| Heuristic | Value |
|---|---|
| Emergency fund | 6 months of essential expenses + EMIs; 3 months if dual stable salaried income; 9–12 months if single income or self-employed |
| Term life cover | 10–15× annual income + outstanding loans − liquid investments |
| Health cover | Family floater ₹10–25L base in metros, plus a super top-up; do not rely only on employer group cover |
| Savings rate | > 20% of take-home |
| Total EMIs | < 40% of take-home (comfort ≤ 35–40%) |
| Card utilisation | < 30%, ideally < 10% |
| Car EMI | ≤ 10–15% of take-home |
| General inflation for planning | 5–6% |
| Education inflation | 10–12% (https://www.kotakmf.com/Information/blogs/education-inflation-india-rising-costs) — medium |
| Medical inflation | 10–14% — **low, unverified** |
| SIP step-up convention | 10% per year |
| Equity long-run return assumption | Show 10–12% with an 8% sensitivity; never guaranteed |
